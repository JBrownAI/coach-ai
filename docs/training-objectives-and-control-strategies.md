Training Objectives and Control Strategies

Core Concept

The appropriate movement-control strategy during resistance training should depend on the primary objective of the training session.

The same exercise can require different execution strategies depending on whether the goal is maximal performance, skill development, hypertrophy, or movement retraining.

Performance Mode

Performance mode prioritizes successfully producing the desired external performance.

The lifter's primary objective is to accomplish the task as effectively as possible.

Examples include:

- 1-repetition maximum testing
- maximal-strength attempts
- explosive power training
- Olympic lifting
- sport-specific performance
- other situations where the external performance outcome is the primary objective

In these situations, the body should be allowed to coordinate the musculature and movement strategy necessary to produce the desired performance.

The objective is not necessarily to consciously isolate individual muscles.

Action Mode

Action mode prioritizes the intended muscular or mechanical action that is being trained.

The external resistance remains important, but moving the resistance is treated as the consequence of producing the desired muscular action rather than the sole objective.

The conceptual sequence is:

Training objective → target musculature → desired muscular/joint action → movement → external resistance displacement

For example, during a hypertrophy-oriented exercise, the goal may be to organize the movement so that the target musculature performs its intended action effectively rather than simply maximizing the amount of weight moved.

When Action Mode May Be Appropriate

Action-oriented execution may be particularly useful during:

- hypertrophy-oriented training
- submaximal strength-development work
- movement retraining
- technique development
- situations where a lifter consistently accomplishes the external task through compensatory strategies

The goal is not necessarily to prevent other muscles from contributing. Human movement requires coordinated action from multiple muscles.

Instead, the goal is to deliberately organize the movement around the musculature and mechanical action that the exercise is intended to train.

Example: Bench Press

A maximal bench press attempt is primarily a performance task.

The lifter's objective is to move the maximum possible load successfully. Performance mode is therefore appropriate.

During submaximal hypertrophy-oriented bench pressing, however, the trainer may use action-oriented cues intended to help the lifter organize the movement around the desired muscular contribution.

The external bar movement remains necessary, but the trainer may emphasize the muscular and joint actions responsible for producing that movement.

Example: Lateral Raise

During a lateral raise performed for hypertrophy, the external task is to move the dumbbell upward.

An action-oriented approach instead emphasizes producing the desired movement of the arm from the shoulder while the dumbbell provides resistance to that movement.

Potential coaching strategies may include:

- reducing the load
- reducing momentum
- controlling the movement
- maintaining torso stability
- emphasizing the desired shoulder action
- avoiding allowing the external load to dictate the movement strategy

Important Distinction From Attentional Focus Research

Action Mode should not automatically be treated as synonymous with "internal attentional focus."

Research on internal and external attentional focus has produced mixed and context-dependent findings.

Action Mode is a broader coaching framework concerning the relationship between:

training objective → movement strategy → muscular action → external performance

Attentional focus is one potential tool used within that framework.

The two concepts should therefore remain separate until further evidence establishes how closely they correspond.

Current Hypothesis

The current CoachAI hypothesis is:

«The optimal movement-control strategy during resistance training depends on the training objective. Performance-oriented tasks should generally prioritize successful external performance, while adaptation-oriented training may benefit from deliberately organizing movement around the intended muscular and mechanical actions.»

This is a working hypothesis rather than an established universal rule.

Evidence Status

Current status: Hypothesis requiring further validation.

Relevant areas of research include:

- internal versus external attentional focus
- motor control
- resistance-training biomechanics
- electromyography
- hypertrophy
- strength development
- motor learning
- movement variability and compensation

Future versions of this framework should be modified if stronger evidence contradicts or substantially qualifies the current model.

CoachAI Implementation Concept

Eventually, CoachAI could first determine:

1. What is the primary training objective?
2. Is the exercise being used for performance, adaptation, skill, or rehabilitation-related purposes?
3. What movement and muscular actions are intended?
4. What execution strategy is currently being used?
5. Is the current strategy appropriate for the stated objective?
6. What is the smallest intervention that could improve execution?
7. What happens after the intervention?

This creates a decision framework rather than simply generating exercise prescriptions.
